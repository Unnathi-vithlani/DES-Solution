Recovery Boiler Advanced Control Solution

Two Opportunities. One Root Cause: Variability.

There are two opportunities for recovery boiler operation improvement: better liquor conversion (increased reduction efficiency), and more process steam from a given quantity of liquor conversion, measured as steaming rate (# steam / # black liquor dry solids). The barrier to both is the same - inconsistent boiler operation and combustion variability.

01

Two Real Opportunities

Better conversion and reduction efficiency is a cost savings opportunity calculated on the reduction of make-up chemicals and the energy needs of unnecessary recycle. Improving the steaming rate (#steam/#BLDS) is quantified as an offset of your most expensive power boiler steam source - two distinct, quantifiable paths to the same bottom line.

02

The Real Barriers

Inconsistent operation and combustion variability show up as poor reduction efficiency, dry stack gas losses, and fouling. Poor reduction and excessive dry stack gas losses are short-term issues. Fouling is the long-term one - reduced heat transfer, downtime, and cleaning costs, and in more severe cases equipment deterioration and damage.

03

Two Sources of Variability

Black liquor variability can often be reduced through better density and temperature control, though many factors remain outside operations' control. Operator variability is the other source - shift-to-shift differences in how each operator responds to changes in combustion, plus longer-term drift, like liquor temperature creeping up ten degrees or more before anyone notices.

04

Beyond Consumed Air

Consumed-air control strategies have been installed successfully in over forty boilers across North America. DES used those principles, then recognized the shortcomings of a fixed air/fuel ratio as process and feed changes altered the liquor and trims maximized out. RecoveryMaxx is our reconfigured, far more capable and robust answer - unmatched by the competition.
